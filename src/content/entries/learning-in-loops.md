It is tempting to divide learning into two stages: first understand, then do. In practice, the boundary is much less tidy.

## Make a small attempt

Reading gives us a vocabulary. Trying something gives that vocabulary a place to live. A small experiment often reveals a question that another hour of reading would never have produced.

The loop is simple: read a little, make a little, notice what happened, then return with a better question.

## Keep the feedback close

Choose an attempt small enough to finish in an afternoon. It might be a diagram, a paragraph, or a tiny program. The goal is to get a response from the material you are working with.

Understanding is often what grows between attempts.
