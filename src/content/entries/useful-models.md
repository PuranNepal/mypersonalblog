A map that included every leaf and every crack in the pavement would be an impressive object. It would also be a terrible map.

Good models make deliberate omissions. They preserve the relationships that matter for a particular question and let the rest fall away.

## Start with the question

Before asking whether a model is accurate, ask what it is for. A walking map and a railway map describe the same city differently because they help us do different things.

The same principle applies to a spreadsheet, a diagram, or a mental picture of how a team works.

## Keep the edges visible

Every simplification has a boundary. A useful habit is to write down three things beside a model:

1. The question it helps answer.
2. The assumptions it depends on.
3. A situation where it might fail.

That short list can be more valuable than another layer of detail.
